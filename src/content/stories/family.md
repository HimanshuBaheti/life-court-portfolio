# Hum Paanch — Five Cousins, One Lifetime of Memories

*Different lives, different cities, and a thousand things going on. But put the five of us together, and nothing really changes.*

There are some people in your life who know you so well that you don't have to explain yourself to them. They know when you're happy, when something is bothering you, and sometimes even what you're about to say before you say it.

For me, those people are my cousins.

We call ourselves **"Hum Paanch."**

Five cousins. Two elder sisters, two elder brothers, and then there's me — the youngest of the lot.

Being the youngest has its own advantages. You get teased a little more, advised a little more, and occasionally treated like you're still a kid, no matter how old you get. But you also have four people who have been looking out for you for as long as you can remember.

After my parents, these four have been my biggest support system.

And the funny thing is, each of them has played such a different role in my life that I can't imagine growing up without any one of them.

### Shreya Jiji — Some Bonds Don't Need an Explanation

If there's one period of my life that I associate very closely with Shreya Jiji, it's the COVID lockdown.

While the rest of the world was struggling with uncertainty, I was fortunate enough to spend a lot of time with her. We studied together, celebrated little occasions, had endless conversations, and somehow found ways to enjoy ourselves even when everything outside had come to a standstill.

We could talk about practically anything. And I think that's what I've always loved about our bond — there's this understanding between us that makes every conversation easy.

But there's one incident I'll never forget.

It was October 2020, around the time of my JEE Advanced examination. My father had contracted COVID, but my family decided not to tell me until after the exam because they didn't want me to lose focus or become worried.

Looking back, I can only imagine how difficult that must have been for everyone.

Throughout that period, my cousins took such good care of me that I never realised what was happening back home.

And then there was the phone.

I still remember the date — **18th October 2020.**

I had been asking Shreya Jiji which phone I should buy. After some research and discussions, we settled on the Samsung M51. She even convinced my mom to let me get it and made sure it was ordered in time.

The phone actually arrived while I was appearing for my exam.

But she hid it from me so that I wouldn't get distracted.

Only after the exam did I get my surprise.

It's such a small thing when I think about it now, but it's one of those memories that has stayed with me. Not because of the phone itself, but because of the thought behind it.

When it was finally time for me to return home, I had so much I wanted to say to everyone. But somehow, I couldn't find the words.

I remember feeling terribly sad about leaving. That night, I couldn't sleep. I even texted Jiji, and she called me to say that she hadn't been able to sleep either.

I think that was when I realised just how attached I'd become.

A few years later, at her wedding, those same emotions came rushing back.

I wanted to be involved in every little thing, to help as much as possible, and to enjoy every moment. And while weddings are full of celebrations, there's always that one moment when it suddenly hits you that things are changing.

For me, it was the final handshake with her and Jijaji.

I remember walking away and struggling to make eye contact because I knew I'd probably start crying.

Later, I wrote her a long message. There was something I'd always wanted her to know.

People sometimes tell me that I wouldn't understand what having a real sister feels like because I don't have one by birth.

But I've never felt that way.

**I have two sisters. Shreya Jiji and Ayushi Jiji. And neither has ever made me feel like anything less than their own brother.**

Shreya Jiji has this beautiful habit of thinking about others without expecting anything in return. She's thoughtful, understanding, and someone I can talk to about almost anything.

And her wedding didn't just give me a new family member. It gave me another elder brother.

### A Jijaji Who Became a Brother

I don't think I've ever felt the need to be particularly formal around Shreya Jiji's husband.

We connected almost immediately.

For starters, he had stayed in the very same hostel where I lived in Pune. That alone gave us plenty to talk about.

But over time, I realised how much we had in common.

We both love cricket, enjoy discussing ideas, and can spend hours talking about business, startups, or practically anything that catches our interest.

What I admire most about him is how calm he is.

No matter what the situation, he somehow manages to think clearly and come up with a sensible solution. He's helped me personally on so many occasions, and I've always appreciated how approachable and genuinely kind he is.

And when it comes to Pune, he's basically a walking Google Maps.

I thought I knew the city pretty well after living there for years, but he still manages to introduce me to places I'd never even heard of.

Somewhere between all those conversations, cricket discussions, and outings, he became one of my go-to people.

I couldn't have asked for a better elder brother.

### Ayushi Jiji — My Pune Companion and So Much More

My bond with Ayushi Jiji is a little different, and honestly, quite difficult to put into words.

We were both living in Pune around the same time, staying in our respective hostels, which gave us something special to share.

Hostel stories.

And anyone who's lived in a hostel knows there's never a shortage of those.

We'd talk about our experiences, the funny things happening around us, and everything in between. We made so many memories during those Pune days, and I always loved having someone from my family nearby who understood that phase of life.

She's incredibly creative, loves reading, and has this amazing amount of knowledge about different things. Her interest in digital marketing and the way she approaches ideas have always fascinated me.

And we share two very important qualities.

**We're both painfully slow eaters. And neither of us seems capable of gaining weight, no matter how much we eat.**

Family meals are particularly entertaining when the two of us are still sitting there long after everyone else has finished.

But jokes apart, Ayushi Jiji has always made sure I never feel left out. There's a comfort in our bond that I can't really explain. We can talk, laugh, share things, and just be ourselves.

When her wedding came around, I had already decided that I wasn't going to miss out on anything.

I was doing my internship at the time, so I took leave to be fully involved in the preparations.

And I'm so glad I did.

There was so much work to be done. Running around, helping with arrangements, clicking countless photos, managing little things, and somehow finding time to enjoy everything in between.

I was exhausted, but in the best possible way.

It turned out to be one of the weddings I enjoyed the most.

And once again, our family grew.

Her husband is genuinely one of the chillest people I've met. Conversations with him are always fun, especially because he has so many ideas about his business.

We often end up discussing how technology and IT solutions could make things easier or help improve different parts of his work.

He brings the business perspective, I bring the technology perspective, and before we know it, we're deep into another discussion.

I love that even as our family has grown, it has brought more people into my life with whom I can share a genuine connection.

### Aaditya Bhaiya — The Brother I Look Up To

Growing up, I've always looked up to Aaditya Bhaiya.

He's the eldest among us, and for as long as I can remember, he's been someone I could turn to for advice, guidance, or just an honest opinion.

He's one of the most positive and straightforward people I know.

And that's something I really appreciate about him.

He doesn't unnecessarily complicate things. If he thinks something is right, he'll tell me. If he thinks I'm making a mistake, he'll tell me that too.

We've both studied engineering, so our conversations often revolve around technology, careers, improving ourselves, and figuring out what to do next.

But it goes much beyond that.

There are things I can discuss with him that I wouldn't necessarily bring up with everyone else. I know he'll listen, understand, and give me his perspective without making it awkward.

He completed his master's in the United States and eventually started working there.

Watching his journey was one of the reasons I started thinking seriously about studying abroad myself.

For a long time, I imagined following a similar path to the US.

Life had slightly different plans, and I ended up in Ireland.

But even then, he continued to guide me.

From understanding what it's like to live away from home to making decisions about studies, careers, and life in general, he's been through so many of the things I'm experiencing now.

Having someone like that to talk to makes a huge difference.

Then came his wedding.

And what a wedding it was.

This time, I was on the groom's side, actively involved in preparations and responsible for taking care of practically everything related to him.

It was probably one of the most exhausting weddings I've ever been part of.

But I enjoyed every bit of it.

There was something happening all the time, something that needed attention, someone calling for help, or some last-minute arrangement to be made.

And amidst all that chaos, we were together.

What I didn't realise then was that **this would probably be one of the last occasions when all five of us would be together for quite some time.**

He was returning to the US, I was preparing to leave for Ireland, and everyone else had their own lives and responsibilities.

I wish I'd known that at the time. Maybe I would've taken a few more photos or spent a little longer sitting and talking with everyone.

His wedding also brought another wonderful person into our family.

My new Bhabhi is a doctor, but that's just one part of who she is.

She's an amazing conversationalist, loves dancing, enjoys anchoring, and has such a creative side. She even has an art page where she shares beautiful acrylic paintings, decorative trays, and other handmade creations.

I've always been impressed by how many different interests she manages to pursue.

We haven't had the chance to spend as much time together as I'd like, but every interaction has been lovely. She's down-to-earth, thoughtful, and someone I genuinely look forward to knowing better.

### Amey — My Partner in Crime

And then there's Amey.

Just one year older than me.

Which means, technically, he's my elder brother.

But does he behave like one?

Not really. And I'm glad he doesn't.

Because we're so close in age, our relationship has always been incredibly easygoing.

We can talk about almost anything, joke around, pull each other's legs, and have conversations that probably wouldn't make sense to anyone listening from outside.

He's one of the most accessible people in my life. There's never any hesitation about calling him or sharing something with him.

He's now started taking responsibility for the family business, which keeps him quite busy.

And I genuinely admire his business mindset.

He has a great sense of humour, understands things quickly, and has this practical way of looking at opportunities and decisions.

Sometimes we talk about serious things, sometimes about completely random nonsense, and sometimes we just end up laughing at each other.

But underneath all that teasing, there's a bond that's difficult to describe.

He's never felt like a cousin to me.

He's always been my brother.

We've stood by each other, shared so many memories, and grown up together in a way that makes the one-year age difference almost meaningless.

And now that he's next in line to get married, I've found a whole new reason to tease him.

Naturally, I'm making the most of it.

### Weddings, Games, and the Joy of Being Together

If there's one thing our family weddings have made me realise, it's how much I love it when all of us are together.

Every wedding has been different.

Shreya Jiji's wedding was emotional for me.

Ayushi Jiji's wedding was the one where I got to contribute, run around, and enjoy being part of everything.

Aaditya Bhaiya's wedding was a beautiful mix of responsibility, exhaustion, excitement, and the realisation that our lives were slowly taking us to different places.

But the best part of all three was always the same.

**Hum Paanch, together again.**

Whenever we meet, we love playing games, chatting, laughing, teasing each other, and revisiting stories that we've probably told a hundred times already.

And somehow, they're still funny.

No matter how much time has passed, it never feels like we need to start over.

We just pick up from where we left off.

And now, with our family growing and three wonderful new people becoming part of our lives, those gatherings have become even more special.

### Five Different Lives, One Constant

As I'm writing this, I'm in Ireland, Aaditya Bhaiya is in the US, and the others are busy with their own work, families, and responsibilities.

Things have changed quite a lot from when we were younger.

We can't meet as often as we used to. Planning a gathering isn't as simple anymore. And sometimes, even finding a time when everyone is free can be difficult.

But there's something comforting about knowing that no matter where I am or what I'm going through, these four people are always there.

I have two sisters who have loved me like their own brother.

An eldest brother who continues to inspire and guide me.

And another brother who's been my companion, my friend, and my favourite person to tease.

Being the youngest of Hum Paanch has meant growing up surrounded by people I could learn from, laugh with, and depend on.

And if there's one thing I wish for, it's that no matter how busy life gets, we never lose this.

That we keep finding reasons to meet, keep planning things together, keep making fun of each other, and keep adding new memories to the ones we already have.

Because when all five of us are together, I don't have to think about how much has changed.

**I'm just the youngest one again, surrounded by my four favourite people.**

And I wouldn't have it any other way.
