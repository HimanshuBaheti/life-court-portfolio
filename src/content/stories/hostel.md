# Hostel Life — The Days I Wish I Could Relive

*Some places become home without you ever realising when it happened.*

It all started in 2020. COVID had turned everything upside down. Exams were getting postponed, there was uncertainty everywhere, and honestly, nobody knew what was coming next. After all the delays and stress, I finally appeared for my MHT-CET, secured admission to PICT in Pune, and began my engineering journey.

Well, technically, I began it from my bedroom.

College had started, but everything was online. Months passed attending lectures through a screen, until one day we finally received the news we'd been waiting for — **college was reopening!**

I was super excited. But there was also this little fear somewhere inside me. For the first time in my life, I was going to leave home, move to a different city, and live on my own. I had no idea what hostel life would actually be like.

That's when my mom told me something I'll probably remember forever.

She had lived in a hostel during her own college days, and when I shared my worries, she said:

**"Son, you will never forget the memories you're going to make in hostel. It will be one of the most cherished phases of your life, and you'll always wish you could live those days again. The friends you make there will be your friends for life."**

At that moment, I didn't fully understand what she meant.

Today, I couldn't agree more.

### A New City, A New Home, and Eight Crazy People

I moved into a community hostel in Pune, which, by the way, was famous for its mess. Four meals a day, and genuinely some of the best hostel food you could find in Pune. Not a bad start, right?

On my very first day, I met quite a few people. New faces, new conversations, everyone trying to figure out their new surroundings. Slowly, friendships started forming, and somewhere along the way, there were these eight guys who became an inseparable part of my everyday life.

We were all different. Different personalities, different backgrounds, different stories, and definitely different levels of craziness. But somehow, we just clicked.

And then there was **that one room**.

Every hostel group probably has one. The room where everyone ends up, no matter what the original plan was. We'd gather there for absolutely no reason, start talking about something random, and before we knew it, hours had passed.

There was laughter, teasing, endless leg-pulling, serious conversations that somehow turned into jokes, and jokes that we would keep bringing up for months.

The funny thing is, I can't even remember half the things we talked about. But I remember how much we laughed.

And without even noticing, that fear of leaving home had completely disappeared.

### The Things We Did (And Probably Shouldn't Have)

Hostel life was never just about attending college, studying, and coming back to sleep.

There was always something happening.

Playing cards, even though they were banned in the hostel. Three people somehow managing to fit on one scooty. Call of Duty matches that became way more competitive than they needed to be. Late-night conversations, shouting across rooms, laughing at the smallest things, and constantly finding new ways to annoy each other.

And then there was our hostel cricket tournament.

Six weeks before my exams, while trying to stop an overthrow, I ended up fracturing my right hand.

Yes, my right hand. Just before exams.

Not exactly my smartest moment.

But here's the important part — **we won the tournament!**

So at least the fracture came with a trophy and a story that my friends will probably never let me forget.

Between college events, hostel celebrations, cricket matches, studying together, and doing absolutely nothing together, there was rarely a dull moment.

We didn't need elaborate plans to have fun. Most days, all we needed was everyone in the same room.

### From Hostel Rooms to Exploring the World

One of the best things about our friendship is that it didn't end when we left the hostel.

Our first big trip together was Rajasthan in 2022. Then came Kashmir in 2023, which remains one of my favourite trips. And in 2026, we took things international with Thailand — easily our best trip together so far.

Three completely different trips, countless stories, and the same people.

From sharing a hostel room to exploring places together years later, it feels pretty special to see how far we've come.

And somewhere during our hostel days, we even earned ourselves a rather interesting nickname from our juniors — **the 1CR Group**.

The reason? All of us had secured placements in good companies, and when our annual packages were added together, the total crossed ₹1 crore.

It sounds funny even now, but I guess that was their way of recognising us.

What I remember more, though, is everything that happened before those placements. The late-night studying, the exam stress, the discussions about interviews, and being genuinely happy whenever one of us had good news to share.

We celebrated each other's wins because, somewhere along the way, they had started feeling like our own.

### A Little More Than Friendship

Living away from home taught me things I never expected to learn.

One of the biggest was managing money. Keeping track of expenses, understanding when to spend and when to save, and slowly learning to be responsible for myself. Nobody sat me down and taught me all this. It just became part of everyday life.

But the most valuable part of those years was the people.

Whenever someone received food from home, it was never really just for that person. The box would open, everyone would gather, and within minutes, there was barely anything left.

If someone needed help, there was always somebody around. If someone was having a bad day, the others somehow found a way to make it better, even if that meant making fun of him until he started laughing.

We stood by each other through exams, placements, celebrations, and the ordinary ups and downs of college life.

We had arrived as strangers, but we slowly became something much closer to family.

### If I Could Go Back for Just One Day...

I don't think I'd choose a big college event, a trip, or even the day we won that cricket tournament.

I'd probably choose a completely ordinary evening.

Everyone sitting in that same room. Someone scrolling through their phone, someone telling a ridiculous story, two people arguing about something pointless, and the rest of us laughing until our stomachs hurt.

No big plans. No responsibilities to think about. Just us being us.

Back then, we never really thought about how quickly those days would pass. We were too busy enjoying them.

And now, whenever I think about hostel life, I remember what my mom told me before I left home.

She was right about everything.

I did make memories that I'll cherish forever. I did find friends I hope to have for life.

And yes, **if I ever got the chance, I'd go back and live those days all over again.**

Not because everything was perfect, but because those imperfect, funny, chaotic, ordinary days turned out to be some of the happiest days of my life.
